## Contract labour under the OSH Code

The Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970 is repealed, and its
rules now sit in the OSH Code.[[code-osh]] The contract labour part applies to
an establishment that employs 50 or more contract labour, or did on any day in
the last 12 months, and to a manpower supply contractor who employed 50 or
more. The 1970 Act started at 20.[[code-osh]][[clra-1970]] Work that is only
intermittent or casual is left out.[[code-osh]]

A contractor covered by this part needs a licence, valid for five years, that
states how many workers it may supply. Using a contractor who needs a licence
and has none is itself a breach of the Code.[[code-osh]]

If the contractor doesn't pay on time, or pays short, the principal employer
pays the workers in full and recovers the amount from the contractor. The
principal employer also provides the welfare facilities the Code requires at
its site.[[code-osh]] A contractor may not charge contract labour any fee or
commission.[[code-osh]]

## Questions

**Is a licence taken under the 1970 Contract Labour Act still valid?**
The 1970 Act is repealed. The OSH Code keeps anything done under it in force,
to the extent it doesn't conflict with the Code, until the Central Government
repeals it.[[code-osh]] Check your state's rules for when a licence under the
Code is needed.

**Does a contractor with fewer than 50 workers need a licence?**
The contract labour part of the OSH Code applies to a manpower supply
contractor who employed 50 or more contract labour on any day in the last 12
months, and to establishments with 50 or more.[[code-osh]] Below that it
doesn't apply. Other duties, such as minimum wages and paying on time, still
do.[[code-wages]]
